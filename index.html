Strategy

What the optimiser decides

Every 30 minutes of the day, the optimiser chooses all of these together, so it can plan the battery and the equipment around the highest billed 30 minutes.

  • Battery: when to charge, when to discharge, and how full it is. It starts the day half full and must end at least half full. Round-trip efficiency is 90%.
  • Shiftable equipment: how much of the flexible load runs in each 30 minutes, with total energy unchanged. With EV chargers set, only the chargers move, and only inside operating hours (Mon to Fri 07:30 to 18:00, Sat 07:30 to 14:00, Sun closed). With no chargers, a share of the 09:00 to 17:00 load moves.
  • Grid: how much to import each 30 minutes, within the grid limit (a hard limit: if it cannot be met, the plan gets as close as it can and says so), with the highest billed 30 minutes as the maximum demand.
  • Solar: all of it is used on site first. No export is assumed.

Goal: lowest bill (energy charge plus maximum demand charge). The baseline is solar only. Cost-saving minimises the bill. Balanced moves half as much equipment, adds a small battery wear cost, and allows only half of the extra CO₂ that Cost-saving would add. Production-first moves no equipment at all, and its battery follows the same CO₂ limit and wear cost as Balanced. The CO₂ limit matters because charging the battery from the grid loses energy: with a 90% battery, about 11 kWh is lost for every 100 kWh discharged.

Not included yet: diesel or gas, individual machines with start and stop rules, real meter history (the demand forecast is trained on synthetic history), export income, forecast uncertainty, the retail charge, the 1.6% KWTBB levy and tax.

Units. kWp is the panel's peak power (a power, in kW). kWh is energy: power × time. A day's solar energy is kWh per day. We never write "kW/h".

String inverter: one inverter serves a whole string of panels, so shading on one panel limits the whole string. Micro-inverter: one small inverter per panel, so it copes better with shading, but costs more. The model does not simulate inverters one by one: its system efficiency of 0.88 covers inverter, wiring, dirt and mismatch together.

How we calculated this
  1. Weather. Hourly temperature, cloud and sunshine for your site from Open-Meteo: a forecast for today and the next 2 days, recorded weather for past days.
  2. Solar. Sunshine is turned into power per kWp: cell temperature = air temperature + (45 − 20) / 800 × sunshine; power = sunshine / 1,000 × (1 − 0.0035 × (cell temperature − 25)) × 0.88. Tilt is ignored. A learned correction is used only if it beat the plain forecast on held-back days.
  3. Demand. Your meter file if you loaded one (real readings on the days it covers, your typical weekday, Saturday or Sunday pattern otherwise). If not, a model by hour, day type and temperature, trained on synthetic history, so treat it as an estimate.
  4. Tariff. TNB rates for your tariff plus your AFA. On a time-of-use tariff the peak rate and the maximum demand charge apply on weekdays 14:00 to 22:00. The billed peak is the highest 30-minute average in that window. A day carries the monthly demand charge divided by 30.
  5. Optimiser. A linear program plans every hour: grid import, battery charge and discharge (round-trip efficiency from your settings, power = half the capacity, starts and ends the day at least half full) and flexible load (09:00 to 17:00, total energy unchanged). It minimises the bill, with a limit on extra CO₂ for the CO₂ strategies.
  6. Baseline and savings. Baseline = solar only, no battery, no load shifting. Saving = baseline cost − plan cost. CO₂ = grid kWh × one flat factor (a placeholder; check it against an official figure).
  7. What it does not do yet. Plan in 30-minute steps (TNB bills the highest 30 minutes), plan across several days, use real demand history unless you upload it, model the retail charge, the 1.6% KWTBB levy or tax, or control any equipment.
